## Lightmeta — AI camera product work

**Product Intern, App · Shenzhen · May–August 2026**

LumaQ is an AI camera app within a hardware business. My work connected that direction to an instant-capture experience: prioritize live effects over a catalogue of templates, then define how those effects behave while shooting and exporting.

### Decisions and delivery

- **Product direction:** compared six competing products and translated the hardware strategy into an experience centered on taking a picture and seeing the effect immediately.
- **Two effects:** led face-aware retro sparkles and hand-tracked star collages from concept to launch. I authored PRDs and coordinated reviews of their capture and relighting interactions; engineering and design delivered their respective implementation work.
- **Camera Control:** led the feature through launch with two distinct paths: launch LumaQ using the hardware control after system setup, and use the control as an in-app shutter. Relighting and printing stayed outside this MVP.
- **Technical communication:** mapped the AI relighting pipeline from model outputs through lighting and shader compositing, and explained the architecture and rendering constraints to product colleagues.

### A public example of my interaction work

[FrameText](https://github.com/Jeffreyliu0131/frame-text-demo) is my independent browser demo for exploring and assessing camera effects. I defined automatic palm tracking to avoid extra gesture setup, and capture-time effect freezing so an export retains the preview composition. AI agents assisted implementation.

FrameText is a personal prototype, not LumaQ's production implementation. The public material contains no employer code or internal documents. The experience above describes my responsibilities and delivery; it does not assert a measured engagement, revenue, or image-quality improvement.

## Penn State — Work-Instruction Voice Assistant

**Industry-sponsored capstone · Three-person team · August–December 2025**

The team explored a voice interface for work instructions. Semester hardware and API constraints led to a laptop prototype instead of the initial glasses-oriented scope.

**My contribution:** led the voice pipeline, integrating Azure speech recognition and synthesis with the language model, including prompt/response handling and playback. I supported the scope change, timed the speech/model/speech stages, and participated in testing.

**Team contribution:** teammates trained the model using sponsor-provided SOP material and owned other project responsibilities. I do not claim that model training as my work or describe the system as retrieval-augmented generation.

**What the result supports:** a team-delivered laptop prototype and a documented integration process. The final report records a mean total of 11.9 seconds across the three timed service calls. That is a team-reported measurement, not time to first audio, a demonstrated improvement, or independently reproduced end-to-end performance.

Sponsor materials and the original team report are not included in this public repository.
