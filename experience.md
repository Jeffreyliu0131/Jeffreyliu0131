## Lightmeta — AI camera product work

**Product Intern, App · Shenzhen · May–August 2026**

LumaQ is an AI camera app within a hardware business. My work connected that direction to an instant-capture experience: define effects that support taking a picture, seeing the result and sharing it immediately.

### Decisions and delivery

- **Scope choice:** deferred dynamic multi-panel and multi-frame cutout ideas because their expected latency and computation cost needed to justify their role in an instant-capture experience.
- **Two effects:** led retro sparkles and hand-tracked star collages from concept to launch. I authored the interactions and PRDs, and aligned five recognition/interaction rules with algorithm colleagues: avoid faces, extinguish stars near the head, flash by region, follow the hand, and recognize collage colors. Engineering and design delivered their respective implementation work.
- **Camera Control:** led the feature through launch with two distinct paths: launch LumaQ using the hardware control after system setup, and use the control as an in-app shutter. Relighting and printing stayed outside this MVP.
- **Technical communication:** mapped the AI relighting pipeline from model outputs through lighting and shader compositing, and explained the architecture and rendering constraints to product colleagues.

[LumaQ’s App Store version history](https://apps.apple.com/cn/app/lumaq-%E7%9B%B8%E6%9C%BA/id6756262410) lists sparkles, collage and Camera Control in **1.3.0** (checked 22 September 2026). This supports the release status; the contribution description above is my account of my responsibilities.

### A public example of my interaction work

[FrameText](https://github.com/Jeffreyliu0131/frame-text-demo) is my independent browser demo for exploring and assessing camera effects. I defined automatic palm tracking to avoid extra gesture setup, and capture-time effect freezing so an export retains the preview composition. AI agents assisted implementation.

FrameText is a personal prototype, not LumaQ's production implementation. The public material contains no employer code or internal documents. The experience above describes my responsibilities and delivery; it does not assert a measured engagement, revenue, or image-quality improvement.

## Penn State — Work-Instruction Voice Assistant

**AI Glasses · Industry-sponsored capstone · Three-person team · August–December 2025**

The team explored a voice interface for work instructions. Semester hardware and API constraints led to a laptop prototype instead of the initial glasses-oriented scope.

**My contribution:** led alpha/beta bot development and the voice pipeline, integrating Azure speech recognition and synthesis with the language model, including prompt/response handling and playback. I supported the scope change, timed the speech/model/speech stages, and participated in testing.

**Team contribution:** a teammate fine-tuned the model using sponsor-provided SOP material and owned other project responsibilities. I do not claim that model training as my work or describe the system as retrieval-augmented generation.

**What the result supports:** a team-delivered laptop prototype and a documented integration process. The team tested nine SOP files over three rounds. Its report records mean total test time of 19.46 seconds in round two and about 11.9 seconds in round three across three timed stages. These are team-reported test results. Fixed waits and incomplete failed-stage timings limit comparability; they do not establish my individual causal impact, time to first audio, or an independently reproduced performance improvement.

Sponsor materials and the original team report are not included in this public repository.
